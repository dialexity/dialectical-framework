### Turns that end on a question, opening vs returning session

Ending on a question is not a defect — most good counsel turns do,
in every arm, so the absolute rate says nothing. The CHANGE does:
near zero means the arm keeps its balance on return, strongly
positive means it flips from advising to interrogating exactly when
the person came back wobbling and needed ground to stand on.

Why the session split and not the `pushback_*` beats: widening
`pressure` to include the in-session pushback turns erases the
separation entirely (r16: A1.7 +0.21 vs A2 +0.29). A2 holds its

where it has something no prompt arm can have, a recorded decision

==============================================================================
Reading this report
==============================================================================

1. Check the validity section FIRST. A collapsed A2 arm or a
   single-tier run bounds what any number below can mean.
2. Read the CI, not the mean. A row whose interval covers zero is not
   a small effect — it is an unmeasured one, and it is compatible with
   an effect in EITHER direction. Measured over the 300 saved delta
   rows: within-dimension sd is ~1.1 rubric steps, so the 95%
   half-width is ~0.63 at n=12 and ~1.25 at n=3. Most gaps this bench
   prints are inside that. Do NOT read a run-to-run movement off two
   means whose intervals overlap; that mistake was made between r15
   and r16 and cost a round of chasing a cause that was not there.
3. A delta only counts if the machine scores agree with the judge.
   Where they disagree, trust the machine score — it cannot be
   flattered by eloquence.
4. 'depreciating' deltas will shrink to zero as models improve; do
   not build the product claim on them. 'durable' deltas are the
   claim. 'absent' means the framework added nothing measurable.
5. On poor-fit controls the framework SHOULD show no gain. A win
   there means the judge is rewarding structure, and the rubric
   needs revision before any other number is trusted.
